# Building the reference keyless works, step by step

The reference is `../reference.py`. All numbers are in millimetres.

## 1. Lay out the trains around the given parts

- **Decision.** The winding train goes above the stem, towards the barrel on the main-plate side.
  The setting train goes below the stem, towards the minute wheel on the dial side.
- **Why.** The two trains must never touch. Putting them on opposite sides of the stem separates
  them by construction.
- **Verify.** At rest, every pair of parts overlaps by less than the tolerance.

## 2. Turn the motion through 90 degrees with a crown wheel

- **Decision.** A 12-tooth winding pinion (module 0.15) on the stem axis drives a 36-tooth face
  (contrate) crown wheel lying in the xy plane. The crown wheel also carries a 12-tooth spur pinion
  (module 0.2) that drives a 36-tooth wheel on the barrel arbor. That gives 9 stem turns per arbor turn.
- **Why.**
  - A face gear meshes with an ordinary spur pinion at right angles.
  - The face teeth are generated by envelope cutting: subtract the pinion from a blank at many
    synchronised angles. The result is conjugate by construction.
  - 9 stem turns per arbor turn gives about 1.8 h of reserve per crown turn. Step 3 needs 0.75 h from
    one turn, so the ratio must stay at or below about 10.7 turns.
- **Trap.** The crown wheel's hub must not reach down into the face-teeth zone, or it rubs the
  winding pinion. (The first version did this: 0.002 mm³ of overlap.)

## 3. Make backward turns harmless with Breguet teeth

- **Decision.** The winding pinion runs free on the round part of the stem. A sliding pinion sits on
  the square part.
  - The two pinions face each other with 8 sawtooth face teeth, 0.15 high: one steep face and one
    gentle ramp per tooth.
  - A light spring pushes the sliding pinion towards the winding pinion (`rest: -1` along +x).
- **Why.**
  - Forward, the steep faces meet. With static friction μ = 0.51 a face inclined more steeply than about
    63 degrees to the sliding direction cannot be cleared by sliding, so the winding pinion is driven.
  - Backward, the ramps meet and the sliding pinion slips axially against its spring.
- **Traps.**
  - Symmetric teeth (two ramps) slip both ways and never wind.
  - Square dog teeth lock both ways: winding works, but backward jams, and the barrel cannot run free.
  - Teeth finer than the step travel can skip.

## 4. Hold the mainspring with a click

- **Decision.** The barrel arbor carries a 24-tooth sawtooth ratchet ring. A click lever has its
  spring urging its tip into the ring.
  - The pivot lies on the tangent through the tip, on the side the mainspring pushes the tooth towards.
  - The click is a `spring` mobile, so it rides up the ramps while winding and drops back.
- **Why.** With the pivot on the tangent, the tooth pushes the tip straight at the pivot. The click
  cannot rotate out of the way, so it locks.
- **Traps.**
  - A click declared `free` is lifted out during winding and never comes back, so step 2 fails.
  - A click declared `fixed` blocks winding.
  - A sliver-thin click never overlaps enough to hold.
  - A ratchet ring without teeth holds nothing.

## 5. Shift to hand setting by pulling

- **Decision.** A collar on the stem sits in a counterbore of the sliding pinion, 0.15 behind the step.
  - Pulling the stem 0.9 drags the sliding pinion 0.75 outwards. That clears the Breguet teeth after
    about 0.3 of pull.
  - From about 0.5 of pull, the sliding pinion's outer 12 spur teeth enter a face-toothed setting
    wheel below the stem.
  - The setting wheel's 12-tooth pinion drives the given minute wheel.
- **Why.** The order is designed so the Breguet teeth let go before the setting teeth engage. The
  middle of the pull (0.2 to 0.4) is fully disengaged, never both.
- **Traps.**
  - Setting teeth too close: the backward slip (0.15 axial) already touches the setting wheel.
  - Stroke too short: never reaches the setting wheel.
  - Spring pointing the wrong way: the pinion drifts off the Breguet teeth.

## 6. Hold everything in place

- **Decision.** Posts with heads support the crown wheel, the setting wheel and the click. There is a
  bridge on the inner side of the winding pinion, and a guide near the case wall for the stem.
- **Why.** Every free or spring mobile, shifted 0.3 off its axis, must hit something.
- **Trap.** The stem guide must stay inside the case radius. Only the stem may cross it.

## 7. Verify by using it, in order

Run the acceptance sequence:

1. wind 1 turn;
2. let go;
3. reserve and free running;
4. backward 360 degrees;
5. pull out with test turns, then set;
6. push back with test turns, then wind again.

The reference achieves every step. Its numbers are in `SOLUTION.md`.
